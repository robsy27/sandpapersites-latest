# Asking for reviews

The review link, the words to use, and when to send them.

```

```

Google's own short link, from "Ask for reviews" in the profile manager. It
opens the review box directly. The long form also works but looks like
phishing in a text message:
`https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=ChIJbZxV-DKTekgRXwGIdy2fB0A`

## The constraint, stated honestly

There is **no back catalogue to mine**. As of November 2026 there are two
clients and two reviews — Daniel Benson at Dan's Cafe, and Bob. Both have
already left one. The profile is at 2 reviews against competitors sitting
on 25 to 600.

So "get to ten reviews" is not an afternoon of asking. It is a consequence
of doing ten jobs. What this document is for is making sure that **every
single job from here produces a review**, because at this volume missing
one costs a measurable fraction of the profile's prominence.

The conversion rate on a well-timed ask is high — most happy clients say
yes. The rate on a badly-timed one is near zero. Timing is the whole thing.

## When to ask

**At handover, in person or on the phone, while they are looking at the
finished site.** Not by email a week later. The moment they say some
version of "that's great" is the moment — they have just felt the thing you
are asking them to describe.

Ask verbally first, then send the link while you are still talking to them.
A link that arrives during the conversation gets clicked. One that arrives
the next morning joins the pile.

**Never offer anything in exchange** — not a discount, not a free month,
not anything. Google removes incentivised reviews and can suspend the
profile. Asking is fine. Paying for it is not.

## What to say out loud

> Glad you're happy with it. Can I ask a favour — would you mind leaving a
> Google review? I'm competing with sandpaper shops for my own name at the
> moment, and reviews are pretty much the only thing that fixes it. Takes
> about a minute, I'll send you the link now.

The reason matters. "It helps my business" is vague and easy to agree with
and forget. "I'm losing to Screwfix when people search my name" is specific,
slightly funny, and people remember it.

## The follow-up message

Send immediately, while you are still with them. Text, not email.

> Thanks again Dan — here's that review link:
> https://g.page/r/CV8BiHctnwdAEBM/review
>
> Anything you write is helpful, but if you can mention Sandpaper Sites by
> name and what I actually built, that's the bit Google pays attention to.
>
> Tom

**Why the name request.** Review text containing "Sandpaper Sites" does two
jobs at once: it adds prominence, and it teaches Google that the two words
are a business name rather than a description of abrasive paper. That second
job is the whole brand-query problem. An ordinary five-star review with no
text helps half as much.

Do not script what they should say beyond that. A review that reads as
dictated is worth less than a short clumsy honest one, and Google is
reasonably good at spotting a pattern.

## If they say yes and nothing appears

One reminder, about four days later, same thread, two lines:

> No rush at all — just bumping this in case it got buried.
> https://g.page/r/CV8BiHctnwdAEBM/review

Then stop. A second chase costs more goodwill than the review is worth.

## Replying to reviews

**Reply to every one, within a day or two.** It is an engagement signal, it
is visible to everyone reading the profile, and it takes two minutes.

Reply in your own voice, name the specific job, keep it short. Do not open
every reply with "Thank you for your kind words" — a column of identical
replies reads as automated and undoes the point.

> Thanks Dan. The order-ahead side of it was the interesting part — glad
> it's earning its keep.

For a bad one, if it ever comes: answer the substance, once, without
arguing, and offer to take it offline. Everyone reading it is judging the
reply, not the complaint.

## Where else to ask

Only after Google. The profile is the one that affects whether people find
the business at all.

Second place is a named quote for the site's own testimonials, which is a
different ask and can be made of the same person at the same time — see
`src/content/testimonials.ts`. Never invent one.
