# Cold outreach notes

Practical constraints on approaching local businesses by email. None of this
is about what to say — it is the stuff that quietly decides whether the
email arrives at all, and the one bit of law that applies.

## Before scaling past a handful a day

**Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set on `sandpapersites.co.uk`.** Without
them, mail from `contact@sandpapersites.co.uk` to a stranger lands in spam,
and you will never find out why — no bounce, no reply, nothing. This is the
single most common reason cold email "doesn't work".

**Cap it at 15–20 a day** from a standing start. A domain with no sending
history that suddenly pushes volume gets treated as a spammer.

## Format

**Plain text beats HTML** for a first email. Your signature is the only
formatting worth keeping.

**No tracking pixel on first contact.** Not worth the deliverability cost.

**If you attach or embed an image, keep it under 100KB and keep it to one.**
Gmail and Outlook block images from unknown senders by default, so anything
essential has to survive the image not loading. Never put the only copy of a
link inside an image.

## The law

Under UK PECR, **sole traders and unincorporated partnerships count as
individual subscribers**, so unsolicited marketing email to them needs
consent. Limited companies and LLPs are corporate subscribers and do not.
A lot of small local businesses are sole traders.

Staying clean is cheap:

- Say plainly who you are and what you do
- Give a way to opt out — one line is enough
- Never email the same business twice if they do not reply, beyond the one
  follow-up below
- **Keep a record of anyone who asks not to be contacted again**, and honour
  it

## Following up

**Once, after about a week.** Same thread, two lines, no new pitch. Then
stop. A third email converts nobody and costs you the domain's reputation.

## Keep a record

One row per business: name, town, how you found them, date contacted, date
followed up, outcome, and whether they asked not to be contacted again.

After forty attempts that record tells you which opening actually works
instead of leaving you guessing — and the do-not-contact column is the part
you are obliged to keep.

## Walking in

Worth saying, because it is the version that works best in a market town:
you are local, the prospects are on one street, and a conversation over a
coffee costs nothing, needs no deliverability, and is not covered by any of
the above.

Do the research first either way. Knowing that a business already owns a
domain, or that their listing has no website on it, turns a cold approach
into a useful observation.
